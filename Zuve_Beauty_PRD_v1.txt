1. Overview
1.1 Project Summary
Zuve Beauty is a direct-to-consumer press-on nail brand in India offering salon-quality, reusable soft-gel nails. This document specifies the requirements for a complete redesign of the Zuve Beauty e-commerce website — built in Next.js using the Antigravity development platform — modelled in look, feel, and editorial structure on Dior Beauty (dior.com/en_us/beauty).
The redesign serves two strategic goals: first, to elevate Zuve Beauty's perceived brand equity from a transactional D2C site to a luxury beauty destination; and second, to systematically resolve the conversion friction identified in the existing CRO audit — most notably around sizing uncertainty, missing trust signals, and a heavy checkout flow.
1.2 Scope
The scope of this v1.0 build is intentionally focused. Three pages will be designed, built, and shipped:
	•	Home Page — the brand storefront, modelled on the Dior Beauty home.
	•	Collection / Landing Page — a curated edit page (e.g. "Soft Feminine" or a seasonal drop), modelled on the Dior Mother's Day fragrance edit.
	•	Product Detail Page (PDP) — the immersive single-product page, modelled on the Miss Dior Body Milk page.

Out of scope for v1.0: cart page, checkout, account/login, blog/journal, search results page, admin/CMS surface. These are referenced where they affect interaction (e.g. "Add to Bag" routes to a slide-out cart that lives on this page) but are not full deliverables.
1.3 Strategic Goals
	•	Brand elevation: position Zuve as the Dior of Indian press-on nails — editorial, considered, premium.
	•	Conversion lift: address every High and Medium priority issue from the existing CRO audit (Section 5 of audit) within the three pages in scope.
	•	Mobile-first parity: ≥60% of Indian beauty traffic is mobile; the experience must feel first-class on a phone, not a scaled-down desktop.
	•	India-context fit: INR pricing, UPI as primary payment cue, COD transparency, free-shipping threshold visible, WhatsApp support trigger.

2. Design Language
DESIGN PRINCIPLE  Restraint over decoration. Whitespace is a feature. Photography is the hero. Typography whispers, never shouts.
2.1 Aesthetic Reference
The design reference is dior.com/en_us/beauty. The aesthetic is editorial luxury: large, generous whitespace; cinematic full-bleed photography alternating with negative space; product compositions photographed as still life; serif headlines paired with a clean sans-serif for utility; a near-monochrome palette punctuated by the natural colour of the products themselves.
Zuve adopts this language but adapts it for press-on nails: the "hero object" is the manicured hand, not a perfume bottle. Where Dior shows a fragrance suspended in motion, Zuve shows hands — close, considered, well-lit, on neutral or rose-tinted backgrounds.

Typography
Display / Editorial: "Cormorant Garamond" (Google Fonts) — used for hero headlines, section titles, the brand wordmark in product callouts. Pairs the high-contrast serif feel of Dior's editorial type. Weight 300 for display, 400 for body when serif is used. 
UI / Body: "Inter" — used for navigation, prices, buttons, descriptions, fine print. Tracking +20 on small caps and labels.
Type scale (rem): 3.75 (display) / 2.5 (h1) / 1.875 (h2) / 1.25 (h3) / 1 (body) / 0.875 (small) / 0.75 (caption). All set in Tailwind config.
Letter-spacing: nav and small-caps labels at +0.08em; display headlines at -0.01em.
Spacing & Layout
	•	8px base grid. Spacing scale: 4, 8, 16, 24, 32, 48, 64, 96, 128 px.
	•	Container max-width: 1440px. Inner content rarely exceeds 1200px; long-form text columns capped at 640px.
	•	Vertical rhythm between sections: 96px on desktop, 64px on mobile.
	•	Asymmetric layouts are encouraged — Dior frequently breaks grid by offsetting a product image into negative space. Replicate this on the Home and PDP.
Imagery & Motion
	•	Photography is always the visual lead. Product shots on neutral seamless paper or rose-tinted backdrops. Hand shots are tightly cropped, hero-lit, with hands in considered (not stock-photo) poses.
	•	No drop shadows on product cards. No gradients except on the rose → bone background fade.
	•	Motion: subtle. Fades and slow translates only. Easing: cubic-bezier(0.22, 1, 0.36, 1). Durations: 300ms (hover), 600ms (section reveal), 1200ms (hero crossfade).
	•	Respect prefers-reduced-motion — disable all parallax and crossfade animation.
2.3 Component Primitives
All components must be built as composable primitives in /components/ui (button, link, input, badge, dialog, sheet, etc.) and styled to match the brand tokens above. Use Radix UI primitives as the unstyled base for accessibility, then style with Tailwind. shadcn/ui is the recommended starting point — install components individually rather than wholesale.

3. Information Architecture
3.1 Site Map (v1.0)
	•	/ — Home
	•	/collections/[slug] — Collection / landing page (e.g. /collections/soft-feminine)
	•	/products/[slug] — Product detail page (e.g. /products/berry-bloom)

All other links in the navigation (Cart, Account, Search) are stubs in v1.0 — they exist visually and are wired to the slide-out cart / a placeholder route, but the destination pages are out of scope.
3.2 Global Header
Sticky header, 80px tall on desktop, 64px on mobile. Background: bone with a 1px gold-tinted hairline at the bottom. Becomes a translucent backdrop-blur on scroll past 100px.
Top utility bar (desktop only): 32px, ink background, bone text, character-spaced. Rotates between three messages every 5 seconds (crossfade): "Free shipping above ₹1,500" / "Reusable up to 15 wears" / "Made in India, shipped worldwide".
Main row: Left — hamburger (mobile) or primary nav (desktop: Shop, Collections, About, Journal). Centre — the ZUVE wordmark in serif, 32px, character-spaced +0.15em. Right — Search icon, Account icon, Cart icon (with item count badge in rose).
3.3 Global Footer
Footer is structured as a four-column grid on desktop, accordion on mobile, with a thin gold hairline above. Background: cream. Columns: (1) Newsletter signup ("Receive the Zuve Edit" + email input + Subscribe button), (2) Find a Boutique [v1: links to retailer locator placeholder], (3) Client Services (Contact, Returns, FAQ, Sizing Guide, Track My Order), (4) Maison Zuve (About, Sustainability, Press, Careers).
Below the columns: a thin row with payment method icons (UPI, Visa, Mastercard, RuPay, Amex, COD), social icons (Instagram, TikTok, YouTube, Pinterest), and "© 2026 Zuve Beauty. India." with a country/language selector.
3.4 Slide-out Cart (Mini-cart)
Triggered from the cart icon. Slides in from the right, 420px wide on desktop, full-screen on mobile. Body: list of line items (image, name, size/colour, qty stepper, price, remove); progress bar showing distance to free-shipping threshold ("You're ₹X away from free shipping"); subtotal; primary "Checkout" button in ink; secondary "View Bag" link. This component directly addresses Section 3.2 of the CRO audit (replace full-page cart with slide-out).

4. Page: Home
REFERENCE  dior.com/en_us/beauty — homepage.
4.1 Page Goal
The Home page introduces Zuve as a brand, demonstrates product quality and breadth, and routes the visitor either into a curated collection (browse intent) or directly into a hero product (buy intent). It must do this without ever feeling like a catalogue.
4.2 Section-by-Section Spec
4.2.1 Hero Carousel
	•	Layout: full-bleed, 80vh on desktop (max 720px), 70vh on mobile. Three slides, auto-advancing every 7s, with crossfade — never slide. Pause on hover.
	•	Content per slide: a cinematic photograph of a hand wearing a featured set, a serif headline left- or right-aligned in negative space (e.g. "For the one you love"), a single text link "Discover" with a hairline underline that animates from 0% to 100% on hover.
	•	Controls: dot indicators bottom-centre (3 dots, 6px each, gold for active), play/pause icon bottom-left, slide counter bottom-right ("01 / 03").
	•	Below carousel: a thin row of three "chapter" thumbnails — each is a tiny 80×60 product image with a one-word label ("Bridal", "Everyday", "Statement"). Clicking jumps the carousel to that slide.
4.2.2 Featured Collection #1 — "The First Press-On"
A full-width section on a bone background. Left half: a striking close-up photograph of hands. Right half: serif headline "The art of effortless nails.", a 2–3 line paragraph in body type, and a "Discover" text link. Vertical alignment is not centred — the headline sits two-thirds down the right column, leaving generous negative space above. Mirrors Dior's "first gloss lipstick" section.
4.2.3 The Zuve Beauty Ritual
Editorial 3-up product spotlight, modelled directly on Dior's "The Miss Dior beauty ritual" block. Layout: large lifestyle photo on the left (40% width), a 3-column product grid on the right (60% width). Each product card is minimal: image only on neutral background, name below in serif, price in Inter, no badges, no buttons. Hover reveals a thin gold underline under the name and a secondary "on-hand" image fade-in (this addresses CRO audit Section 2.2 — secondary hover image).
Below the grid: a single centred ghost button "Discover the ritual →" linking to the relevant collection.
4.2.4 Hero Story Banner
A full-bleed, 70vh editorial banner — a single dramatic photograph (e.g. nails on a draped silk background in a deep rose tone) with a serif headline overlaid: "A celebration of the considered hand." The mood here echoes Dior's "La Collection Privée" iconic-signatures block. Single text-link CTA "Explore". Parallax: the background scrolls at 0.7x page speed (disabled on prefers-reduced-motion).
4.2.5 New Drop / Seasonal Collection
Two-column layout: left column is a lifestyle photo of the new collection in context, right column is a 3-product grid (same minimal card pattern as 4.2.3). Above the grid, a small-caps eyebrow label "NEW THIS SEASON" in gold, then the headline. Below the grid: "Discover" ghost button.
4.2.6 Brand Story Banner
A full-bleed image with the wordmark integrated into the photograph — for example, the Zuve logotype embossed on packaging, photographed as still life. Mirrors Dior's "Dressed in Dior" Sauvage block. Centred headline, single text-link "Read the story".
4.2.7 The Finishing Touch (Triptych)
3-column section on bone. Left column: text — small-caps eyebrow "THE FINISHING TOUCH", serif headline (e.g. "Perfection is in the details."), a 3-line paragraph, and a "Discover" link. Centre and right columns: two tall portrait-ratio images, each captioned with a one-line label below in small-caps ("The art of gifting" / "The personal touch"). Replicates Dior's bottom-of-home triptych.
4.2.8 Pre-Footer Promotional Strip
Three equal columns on a cream background, separated by thin gold hairlines. Each column has a small-caps title and a one-sentence pitch with a text link: (1) "On the House: New Exclusive Offer" — first-order discount; (2) "Celebrate Her with Zuve" — gifting CTA tied to the active collection; (3) "New Arrivals Are Here" — link into the latest drop. This mirrors Dior's pre-footer strip exactly.
4.3 Behaviour Notes
	•	All text-link CTAs ("Discover", "Explore") use a hairline underline that animates left-to-right on hover. Never use a filled button on the homepage except in the slide-out cart. The visual hierarchy belongs to the photograph, not the button.
	•	Section reveal: each section fades in with a 12px upward translate when its top edge crosses 85% of the viewport. Use IntersectionObserver, not scroll listeners.
	•	All images use Next.js <Image> with priority on the hero, lazy on everything below the fold. Provide explicit width/height to prevent layout shift.

5. Page: Collection / Landing
REFERENCE  dior.com/en_us/beauty/page/mothers-day-fragrance.html
5.1 Page Goal
A collection landing page is a curated edit, not a search results page. It sells the story of a theme ("Soft Feminine", "Bridal Edit", "Mother's Day") through alternating editorial photography and product modules. Browse must feel like leafing through a magazine, not scrolling a catalogue.
5.2 Section-by-Section Spec
5.2.1 Collection Hero
A 60vh full-bleed photograph (e.g. a hand wearing the headline set, surrounded by petals or styled props that match the collection's mood). Centred over the image: small-caps eyebrow "THE COLLECTION", serif headline (the collection name, e.g. "Soft Feminine"), and a one-line italic descriptor below the headline. No CTA — the hero is purely atmospheric.
5.2.2 Editorial Intro Block
Below the hero, on a bone background: a centred 640px-wide column of editorial copy. Three short paragraphs telling the story of the edit — the inspiration, the shapes, the colour story. Italic pull-quote at the end. Generous vertical padding (96px top and bottom). This is Dior's "discover" introduction equivalent.
5.2.3 Hero Product Spotlight
A single "signature" product from the collection, presented as a 2-column block: large product photograph on the left, on the right a serif product name, a 2-line description, the price in Inter, and a primary "Discover" link. This is the editor's pick of the collection.
5.2.4 Product Grid #1 — Core Edit
A 3-column responsive grid on desktop, 2-column on tablet, 1-column on mobile. 6 to 9 products. Each card uses the minimal-card pattern (defined in 7.1) with hover behaviours including the secondary "on-hand" image and a Quick Add button (CRO audit: Quick Add to Cart on PLP). Above the grid: small-caps section label and a one-line headline. The grid is asymmetric — every third card spans 1.5x the height to break rhythm, mirroring how Dior breaks its grid with featured tiles.
5.2.5 Editorial Break — Lifestyle Story
A full-bleed lifestyle photograph (a styled scene, not a product shot — e.g. an open vanity, hands holding flowers, a mood board) with a centred serif headline overlay (e.g. "A note for her.") and a one-line italic caption. No CTA. This is purely a pacing device — it gives the eye a rest between product modules and tells the reader that this is an edited experience, not a feed. Mirrors Dior's mid-page "Give her something unexpected" break.
5.2.6 Product Grid #2 — Companion Pieces
Smaller 4-column grid, more product-dense, no asymmetric breaks. These are the supporting products in the edit — accessories, refills, related shades. The visual treatment is slightly more catalogue-like to signal "add-on" rather than "hero". 8 to 12 products.
5.2.7 Gifting Block
A 2-column block: left, a beautifully styled photograph of one of the collection sets in gift packaging; right, a vertical stack — small-caps eyebrow "THE ART OF GIFTING", serif headline, 3-line paragraph, and a "Discover gifting →" text link. Echoes Dior's "The most generous gift" block.
5.2.8 Editorial Outro / Sign-off
A 3-line italic serif paragraph, centred, on a cream background, max-width 560px. The sign-off — a small piece of editorial closing copy that puts a bow on the collection. Below it, a thin gold underline-link to the next related collection ("Continue to: Bridal Edit →").
5.3 Filter / Sort Behaviour (Optional v1.0)
Filter and sort are out of scope for v1.0 because a curated collection page is, by intent, pre-curated. If a filter UI is included, it lives in a discrete "Refine" button at the top of the first product grid that opens a left-side sheet — never a horizontal toolbar. Default sort is the editor's curated order. Do not implement filters in v1.0 unless explicitly requested; the page should feel like an article, not a category.

6. Page: Product Detail (PDP)
REFERENCE  dior.com/en_us/beauty/products/miss-dior-body-milk-Y0997169.html
6.1 Page Goal
The PDP must do three things, in order of priority: (1) eliminate the sizing uncertainty that the CRO audit identified as the #1 friction point; (2) build trust through reviews, UGC, and craft language; (3) make the add-to-bag action one tap away at all times — including on mobile via a sticky bar. The page is also the most editorial of the three — it tells the story of one product across the full vertical scroll, in the spirit of how Dior treats Miss Dior Body Milk.
6.2 Above-the-Fold: Gallery + Buy Box
6.2.1 Layout
Two-column desktop layout, 60/40 split. Left column: gallery. Right column: buy box, sticky to the top of the viewport so it stays visible as the gallery scrolls. On mobile, the gallery becomes a horizontal swipe carousel above the buy box, and the buy-box CTA becomes a sticky bottom bar (CRO audit medium-priority item).
6.2.2 Gallery
Left column: a 4-up vertical thumbnail strip on the far left (each 80×80, 8px gap), and a large primary image to its right. Click thumbnail to swap the primary image. Pinch-to-zoom on mobile; click-to-zoom (modal lightbox) on desktop. Image count: minimum 5 — primary product on neutral background, product in packaging, on-hand close-up, full-hand wide shot, and a video thumbnail (10–20s loop of the application process). The video thumbnail has a centred play icon and opens an inline player.
6.2.3 Buy Box
Right column, top-aligned, sticky. From top to bottom:
	•	Small-caps category breadcrumb ("SOFT GEL NAILS").
	•	Product name in serif, 2.5rem, weight 400.
	•	One-line italic descriptor ("Reusable soft-gel nails with a rose-pink finish.").
	•	Star rating — 5 stars rendered as filled/empty SVG, plus a numeric rating and review count to the right ("4.8 — 124 reviews"). Clicking jumps to the reviews section.
	•	Price in Inter, 1.5rem, with strikethrough original price beside it if discounted, in mute. Tax-inclusive note in small-caps below: "INCLUSIVE OF ALL TAXES".
	•	Trust strip — three small icons in a row (Cruelty-Free / Made in India / Reusable up to 15 wears) with one-word labels. (CRO audit: trust badges near ATC.)
	•	Size / shape selector — a pill-style segmented control with options like "Almond", "Squoval", "Coffin". Selected pill uses ink background and bone text.
	•	Sizing CTA — a prominent text link with a small ruler icon: "Find my size →". Clicking opens the Sizing Guide modal (see 6.3). This is the audit's #1 priority.
	•	Quantity stepper — a horizontal − / [1] / + control.
	•	Primary CTA: "Add to Bag — ₹X,XXX" — a full-width button in ink with bone text. Hover: slight darken. Pressed: 1.5px inward shadow. After click: button text crossfades to "Added ✓" for 1.5s, then the slide-out cart opens.
	•	Secondary text link below the button: "Express Payment" with UPI / Razorpay logos.
	•	A delicate hairline divider, then a single line: "Free 2-day shipping on orders above ₹1,500."
	•	"Mother's Day GIFTING" callout (or active campaign) — a small cream card with a one-line offer message, optional gift wrapping toggle, and a "Learn more" text link. Mirrors Dior's gifting callout in the buy box.
	•	Three thin accordion rows below the buy box: "Ingredients / Materials", "How to apply", "Shipping & Returns". Each opens with a 200ms ease.
6.2.4 What is removed — explicitly
No mandatory "I agree to terms" checkbox before Add to Bag. This is the audit's high-priority fix. Replace it with passive copy at the final checkout step: "By placing your order, you agree to our Terms." This is non-negotiable — do not re-introduce a pre-cart checkbox under any styling.
6.3 Sizing Guide Modal
A centred modal, max-width 720px, opens on "Find my size →". Dimmed backdrop (rgba(26,26,26,0.4)) with backdrop-blur. Three tabs across the top:
	•	Tab 1 — How to Measure: a step-by-step illustrated guide with a printable PDF ruler download link, a "coin reference" photo (₹10 coin = 27mm), and a 30-second video showing the measurement technique. (CRO audit high-priority fix.)
	•	Tab 2 — Size Chart: a clean numeric table — column 1 finger position (Thumb / Index / Middle / Ring / Little), columns 2-16 the 15 included sizes mapped to mm width.
	•	Tab 3 — Fit Quiz (optional): 4-question quiz that recommends a starting nail shape and a standard size template. Saves the result to localStorage so the recommendation persists across sessions.
The modal is keyboard-accessible: Esc to close, Tab traps focus, focus returns to the "Find my size →" link on close.
6.4 Below-the-Fold: Editorial Story Sections
6.4.1 Description Block
A centred 640px column. Editorial product description in serif body — 3 to 5 short paragraphs with a single italic pull-quote in the middle. "See more" link to expand if longer.
6.4.2 "How to Apply" Visual Walkthrough
Two-column block: left is a hand-held photograph of the product mid-application; right is a centred eyebrow label "HOW TO APPLY", a serif sub-headline ("As a finishing touch."), and a 4-step numbered list — each step is one short sentence. Mirrors Dior's "How to apply" section. On scroll, the steps reveal one-by-one with a 100ms stagger.
6.4.3 The Ritual / Story Block
A full-bleed editorial photograph (e.g. florals, packaging, a styled hand) on the right, a vertical text block on the left over a bone or cream background. Eyebrow "THE RITUAL", serif headline ("The rose beauty ritual."), 2-3 line paragraph telling the brand story behind the product. No CTA. Echoes Dior's "The rose beauty ritual" block.
6.4.4 Routine / Cross-Sell Strip
A horizontal scroll strip of 5–7 "Refresh / Hydrate / Add the finishing touch"-style cards. Each card has a minimal product image, a one-word step label ("Step 1 / Step 2 / Step 3"), the product name, and a small "Discover" link. Replicates Dior's "Refresh your skin / Hydrate your skin / Add the finishing touch" routine row. Slot dot indicators below the strip.
6.4.5 Lifestyle Video Block
A full-bleed muted autoplay video (max 30s, looped, with a poster frame) of the product in a lifestyle moment — for example, a slow pan over hands at a café, or a model adjusting a dress with the manicure visible. Centred serif overlay text. Audio toggle bottom-left. Echoes Dior's "And you, what would you do for love?" lifestyle video. Disable autoplay on prefers-reduced-motion and on data-saver mode.
6.4.6 "Discover" Cross-Sell Carousel
A 4-up product carousel on a bone background, with a centred serif headline "Discover". Same minimal card pattern as the homepage. Arrow navigation on the sides; horizontal swipe on touch. This addresses the "empty review section feels like a ghost town" issue obliquely — even if reviews are sparse, the page never feels empty because cross-sell follows.
6.5 Reviews Section
6.5.1 Structure
Section title "PRODUCT NAME — REVIEWS" in serif, centred. Below: a 4-column block — column 1 is a moderation note ("Reviews are moderated by our service partner Bazaarvoice. Read terms."); columns 2-4 contain the review count, sort dropdown, and a "Write a review" button. Each review card: rating, reviewer initials, location, date, headline, body, and a single "Yes, recommends this product ✓" line. Photo reviews appear with a thumbnail strip above the body. Pagination: "Load more" button at the bottom — never an infinite scroll, because the user must be able to reach the footer.
6.5.2 Empty State
Critical: if a product has fewer than 3 reviews, do not render the reviews section as empty (the audit's "ghost town" problem). Instead, render a single editorial card: a serif quote ("This shade is the one I reach for first.") attributed to the founder or a stylist, plus a "Be the first to review" CTA. The empty state must look intentional, not absent.
6.5.3 UGC Strip
Below the reviews, a horizontal strip of Instagram UGC tiles (8 tiles on desktop, swipeable on mobile) under the heading "As worn by you". Each tile is a square image with a small Instagram icon overlay; clicking opens a lightbox with the full post and a "Shop this look" link. (CRO audit: UGC section as social proof.)
6.6 Mobile-Specific Behaviour
	•	Sticky add-to-bag bar: a 64px bar fixed to the bottom of the viewport when the user scrolls past the buy box. Contains: thumbnail of selected variant, product name (truncated), price, and a primary "Add to Bag" button. The bar appears with a 200ms slide-up.
	•	Sticky size selector: when the sticky bar is visible, tapping it expands an inline drawer with the size selector before adding to bag. Don't take the user away from the page.
	•	Gallery: horizontal swipe carousel with dot indicators. Pinch-zoom enabled. Maximum 5 swipes from end-to-end before reaching CTA.
6.7 WhatsApp Support Trigger
A floating circular WhatsApp button, 56px, bottom-right, 24px from edges. On click, opens a wa.me link with a pre-filled message ("Hi, I have a question about [Product Name]"). Hidden when the mobile sticky add-to-bag bar is visible (to avoid overlap), or repositioned to bottom-left in that state. Addresses CRO audit Section 4 — WhatsApp integration.

7. Shared Components
7.1 Product Card (Minimal)
The minimal product card is the most reused unit on the site. Its restraint is what makes the photography breathe.
	•	Square aspect ratio for the image (1:1) on a neutral background — no badges over the image, ever.
	•	Below the image: product name in serif (1rem), one-line shade or shape descriptor in small-caps mute (0.75rem), price in Inter (1rem).
	•	Hover: secondary image crossfades in (300ms); a thin gold hairline animates under the product name; a "Quick Add" button slides up from the bottom of the image at 80% opacity (CRO audit medium-priority).
	•	No drop shadow. No card border. No "Sale" sticker — instead, if discounted, show original price strikethrough in mute beside the new price.
	•	Click target is the entire card, not just the name.
7.2 Quick Add Button
Appears on hover (desktop) and is permanently visible on mobile (small icon-only button bottom-right of the image). Click adds the default variant to the bag and triggers the slide-out cart. If the product has variants (size, shape), Quick Add instead opens a tiny popover with a variant selector — never adds an unselected variant silently.
7.3 Buttons
	•	Primary: ink fill, bone text, no border-radius (0px), no shadow. Padding 16px 32px. Hover: bg lightens by 10%. Reserved for ATC and Checkout.
	•	Ghost: transparent fill, ink border 1px, ink text, no border-radius. Same padding. Hover: bg becomes ink, text becomes bone. Used for "Discover" CTAs in homepage modules.
	•	Text-link with hairline: ink text, 1px gold underline. Hover: underline animates left-to-right via a CSS transform-origin trick. The default editorial CTA throughout the site.
7.4 Announcement Bar
32px height, ink background, bone text. Sits above the header. Rotates between three messages every 5s with a crossfade. Dismissible by a small × on the right (state stored in localStorage so it doesn't reappear in the same session). Mobile: same height, single message at a time, swipeable.
7.5 Newsletter Signup
Used in the footer and as a homepage band before the footer. Inline layout: a one-line label ("Receive the Zuve Edit"), an email input with a hairline bottom border (no box), a "Subscribe" text-link button, and a tiny privacy line below. On submit, the input swaps to a serif confirmation: "You're on the list. Thank you."
7.6 WhatsApp FAB
See Section 6.7. Spec is consistent across all three pages — present always, repositioned on mobile when the sticky CTA bar is showing.
7.7 Slide-out Cart
See Section 3.4 for spec. Lives globally, available from any page. Component name: <CartSheet />. State managed via Zustand store /lib/store/cart.ts.

8. Technical Stack
8.1 Framework & Core
	•	Next.js 15 (App Router) — server components by default, client components opt-in for interactivity.
	•	React 19 — for Actions, useFormStatus, useOptimistic where relevant.
	•	TypeScript 5 — strict mode on. No `any` without justification. All component props typed.
	•	Node 20 LTS on the Antigravity build environment.
8.2 Styling & UI

10. Accessibility & Performance
10.1 Accessibility Standards
Target: WCAG 2.2 AA across all three pages. The site sells a beauty product to a wide audience including older shoppers, screen-reader users, and shoppers using zoom and high-contrast modes. Accessibility is a brand standard, not a checklist.
	•	All interactive elements keyboard-reachable in a logical order; visible focus rings (2px gold offset).
	•	Colour contrast: ink on bone passes AAA; mute on bone passes AA only — never use mute for primary content.
	•	Every image has a meaningful alt attribute; decorative images have alt="".
	•	Headings strictly hierarchical: one h1 per page, no skipped levels.
	•	Modals (sizing guide, lightbox) trap focus, restore focus on close, and can be dismissed with Escape.
	•	Carousels expose previous/next buttons with aria-labels; current slide announced via aria-live="polite".
	•	All forms have visible labels (not placeholder-as-label) and inline error messages tied to inputs via aria-describedby.
	•	Honour prefers-reduced-motion — no parallax, no autoplay, no hero crossfade.
10.2 Performance Budgets
	•	LCP under 2.5s on a Moto G4 / Slow 4G test profile (the realistic Indian mobile condition).
	•	CLS under 0.05; every image has explicit width/height.
	•	INP under 200ms; carousels and modals must not block the main thread.
	•	Total JS shipped per page under 150KB gzipped; lean on server components.
	•	Images: AVIF first, WebP fallback, JPEG ultimate. Hero served at multiple breakpoints (sizes attribute set correctly).
	•	Fonts: subset Cormorant Garamond and Inter, served with font-display: swap, preloaded.
	•	No third-party scripts above the fold; defer GA, PostHog, and chat scripts until after the first interaction or 3s, whichever is first.
10.3 SEO
	•	Each page has unique title and meta description, set via Next.js generateMetadata.
	•	Product pages emit Product schema (JSON-LD) including offers, price, availability, aggregateRating.
	•	Collection pages emit ItemList schema.
	•	Open Graph and Twitter card tags set per page; OG image is the page hero, generated at 1200×630.
	•	XML sitemap auto-generated; robots.txt allows everything except the cart routes.
